## ADR-0004 — Determinism rules
- **Status:** accepted with changes (2026-11-08, founder sign-off)
- **Decision:**
  - **Truths is stateless and hash-derived:** `value = hash(worldSeed, truthId, entityId, index)`. There is no sequence and no locking. Any truth can be queried lazily at any time (a character created later, an arriving rival, a secret resolved in year 20), in any order, and it survives rewinds by construction. The hash is a SplitMix64-style 64-bit mix over the inputs, specified in `sim/Core/TruthRng.cs`.
  - **Dice is stateful:** PCG32 substreams, one per system, each seeded from `hash(diceSeed, systemId)`. A rewind reseeds `diceSeed` and therefore every substream. Generator states and draw counters are serialisable and part of every snapshot.
  - Draw tags are enums with fixed numeric values (`TruthId`, `DiceId`, `DiceSystem`), never strings. Truth ids feed the hash: never renumber or reuse one.
  - Simulation state uses integers. No floating point in state or decisions.
  - Never `System.Random`; never wall-clock time in the sim.
  - Never `string.GetHashCode()` (it is randomised per process). Stable string hashing is FNV-1a 64 (or xxHash).
  - Never iterate a `Dictionary` or `HashSet` where order affects an outcome; use lists ordered by stable ids.
  - Player input is a tick-stamped command queue. Pending commands and the log of applied commands are persisted in saves and snapshots. Same seed + same commands = same world; tests compare state hashes, including snapshot → restore → continue against a straight run.
- **Consequences:** Floats are allowed in the Godot view only. Adding a draw to one system cannot shift another system's sequence.

## ADR-0005 — Time model
- **Status:** accepted (2026-11-08, founder sign-off)
- **Decision:**
  - Calendar: 300 ticks per in-game hour, 24 hours per day, 10 days per season, 4 seasons per year (7,200 ticks per day, 288,000 per year), set in `content/world.json`. Needs, mood and memory decay run hourly; movement and job progress per tick.
  - Base tick rate: 20 ticks per real second at 1× (speeds ×1, ×3, ×6 = 20, 60, 120 ticks/s). A day lasts 6 real minutes at 1× (target 5–8).
  - CPU budget: on average at most 2 µs per character per tick, amortised over a day (hourly and job-start work included), target 1 µs. A benchmark test fails above budget. At budget, a 100-world, 1-year, 10-character soak costs at most 576 CPU-seconds (about 1.2 minutes on 8 cores).
- **Consequences:** Worlds run in parallel on separate threads (each world is single-threaded and self-contained).

## ADR-0006 — Event record and story detection
- **Status:** accepted (2026-11-08)
- **Decision:** Every event carries: id, tick, system tag, localisation key and arguments, actor ids, cause event ids, and a list of persistent effects (grudge formed/ended, debt formed/repaid, death, departure, recruitment, opinion crossing a threshold). The story analyzer walks cause links backwards from each event with persistent effects and counts a story when the chain has ≥3 events, ≥2 distinct systems and a named character. Each terminal event counts once.
- **Consequences:** Systems must record causes faithfully; a missing cause link silently shortens stories, so tests check causal chains for key interactions.

## ADR-0013 — M0 tuning constants live in code (for now)
- **Status:** accepted for M0 (2026-11-08); proposal for founder review. Values updated 2026-11-09 after soak probing.
- **Context:** Some numbers are tuning, not content. Putting them in `/content` needs new schema fields, which needs approval.
- **Decision:** Keep them as named constants (`NeedsSystem`, `SocialSystem`, `Places`, `BloodJobs`, `CombatSystem`) during M0 so soak tuning can move fast. Proposal: after the M0 soak settles the values, add a `tuning` block to `world.json` and its schema.
- **Current values:**
  - Conversation: start 25‰ per 30-tick check (400‰ when seeking company); insult 30‰ base, +250‰ in a low mood (< 25), +300‰ toward a grudge target; deep talk 300‰. (Started at 60‰ / 50‰; probes logged ~400 insults per 40 days, which drowned every other story.)
  - Needs: hungry < 300, starving < 150, fed ≥ 450, collapse < 150, recover ≥ 300. Starvation costs 10 health per hour, so starving lasts days, not hours, and leaves room for rescue, theft and departure.
  - Departure: opinion of You ≤ −45 with a grudge or hunger; or starving for 24 h with mood < 20; or two active grudges with mood < 20.
  - Food: 12 berry bushes (3 clusters of 4), at most 6 berries each, regrowing 2 per day, none in winter. Winter opens with an event that becomes the cause of the hunger that follows. (9 bushes starved every camp; 16 bushes at 2 per day meant nothing ever happened.)
  - Sun: "sun danger" runs from one hour before dawn until dusk. A vampire outside is pulled off any job to seek cover; a vampire on watch stops at sun danger.
  - Blood: after a refusal a vampire waits 2 h before asking anyone else. Donors refuse while hungry, weak (health below max(600, their risky-work threshold + 100)), in a low mood (< 35), or when their opinion of the vampire is below their consent bias. Each reason has its own log line and cites its cause.
  - Incidents: night thief 12‰ per night hour × pressure / 50, from day 3; food spoils 120‰ at 06:00 × pressure / 50 when the stockpile holds ≥ 4 berries; majors at least 6 h apart and cost 30 pressure.
  - Added 2026-11-09 (ADR-0014–0017): insults +4‰ per point of the speaker's dislike of the listener and +60‰ per point of bad chemistry; chats warm opinion by (100 + 50 × chemistry)%; newcomers (first 7 days after arriving) leave at an opinion of You 30 points higher (−15); wanderers arrive on days 1–6 (four) and 29–37 (three, shuffled with Dice); winter frost strips the bushes, then one berry per bush every 2 days.

## ADR-0014 — Promises only when fulfilment is uncertain
- **Status:** accepted for M0 (2026-11-09), lead's call; for founder review.
- **Context:** Every wanderer was promised a meal on joining and ate within the hour, so 700 of the first soak's 1,428 "stories" were the same instant promise-kept chain. A debt opened and closed in an hour is not a story, and it hid that real stories ran at ~7 per world.
- **Decision:** A newcomer is promised only what the camp cannot give right now: a meal when nothing in the stockpile is free for them to eat (amended 2026-11-09 after review: "less than a meal per mouth" still let promises close within minutes); for vampires, a sound roof against the sun when no roof section is mended. Otherwise they are simply welcomed (a Recruitment, not a story by itself). A promised meal is a first claim on food: if food is shared out and the creditor, hungry, is left out, the promise breaks at once (as in the demo, the ration policy decides whether you keep your word). A promised roof is kept when anyone mends a section in time, and repairs made while such a promise is open cite it. The newcomer's food cause becomes the promise, so the hunger that follows chains back to it.
- **Consequences:** "Broken promise → departure" appears in 38% of soak worlds. Kept promises stay open 1 to 40 hours (median about 15); none closes within the hour. Promised rest is no longer kept by sleeping in the ruins. The promise's length comes from world.json (`promiseDueHours`) and reaches the text as "within {n} days" / "within a day".

## ADR-0015 — Rationing that bites, and a winter squeeze instead of a cliff
- **Status:** accepted for M0 (2026-11-09), lead's call; for founder review. Pacing change: the last three wanderers now arrive near winter.
- **Context:** Reservations existed in 39 of 57,600 world-hours: food delivered between hourly checks went to whoever walked past first, so the ration policy (the player's main lever) almost never mattered, and meal theft (a demo regression story) happened in 1 of 100 worlds. Winter was a cliff: bushes kept their berries into winter, famine hit in the last three days, and "went without so others could eat" fired even when nobody ate.
- **Decision:** Food reaching the stockpile is shared out at once by the policy. With empty stores nobody "goes without". The Desperate, left out while critically hungry, go straight for a set-aside meal (the theft itself still happens at the stockpile, if the meal is still there). Winter frost strips the bushes, which then trickle one berry every two days. Four wanderers arrive in the first week; the last three arrive on days 29–37 in an order shuffled with Dice (new draw tag `DiceId.ArrivalOrder`).
- **Consequences:** Winter is a ten-day half-rations squeeze in which the policy decides who eats. Meal theft → grudge appears in 67% of soak worlds.

## ADR-0016 — Pair chemistry is a Truth; dislike feeds insults
- **Status:** implemented provisionally (2026-11-09); **needs founder approval** because it adds a Truth draw (`TruthId.PairChemistry`), which touches the determinism surface (additively: no existing draw changes).
- **Context:** Insults landed on whoever was nearest and chats nudged every opinion up, so opinions converged on mild liking and grudges formed in 6 of 100 worlds.
- **Decision:** Each pair has a chemistry from −2 to +2 (10/20/40/20/10%), a stateless Truth keyed by the pair's cast keys: symmetric, order-independent, rewind-proof, so a player who dies learns who clashes with whom. Bad chemistry adds 60‰ per point to insult odds; chemistry scales the warmth of a chat by (100 + 50 × chemistry)%. Insult odds also rise 4‰ per point of the speaker's dislike of the listener, so feuds feed themselves. Newcomers in their first week leave at an opinion 30 points higher.
- **Consequences:** Grudges form in 88% of worlds. Of 227 grudges in a 100-world soak, 115 came from meal theft, 49 from broken promises, 7 from abandonment in a fight, 2 from refusals and 54 from insults.

## ADR-0017 — Counting and telling stories honestly
- **Status:** accepted for M0 (2026-11-09).
- **Decision:**
  1. One arc is one story: a story whose ending lies inside another story's chain (a broken promise inside "...broke the promise, held a grudge, left") is not counted again.
  2. The headless runner has a stand-in player that sets one of the three ration policies on the first tick, chosen by seed, so a soak exercises every policy's stories. `run` and `soak` share it.
  3. The story log folds a run of identical steps that differ only in one person ("… and the same with Bram, Nia, Mira, until Day 40, 04:00"), and uses singular item names for one.
  4. The player speaks in second person: `World.PlayerId` names the player, and event text picks `<key>_you_<arg>` templates when the player fills that placeholder ("You were hurt", "Mira tended your wounds"). Rival otherworlders, later, are not the player.
- **Consequences:** The honest count is 8.5 stories per world-year after the ADR-0018 cause fixes (it was 9.1 before them, 10.3 with nested sub-stories), from 14 distinct system sets; every world has at least 4. This is below the M0 plan's target of 10, and stays reported as such.

## ADR-0018 — M0 exit check: cause honesty, hooks, and content the code relies on
- **Status:** accepted (2026-11-09).
- **Context:** The exit check gave four fresh readers one raw story log each (all four retold a specific causal story unprompted) and had three reviewers audit determinism (no findings: 12,880 snapshot-restore checks, serial and parallel soaks byte-identical), architecture and story counting.
- **Decision (fixes made):**
  1. An event cites only what brought it about. An insult cites a grudge, else (only when the speaker's mood is low enough to count) their worst thought, else their worst thought about the target, else nothing. "Went without" cites the ration policy only when hungriest-first would have fed that person. A sunburn cites earlier harm only if it is still going on (starving now, or burnt in the last hour). A departure cites food only if the leaver is hungry. Winter is the food cause only for people who actually get hungry.
  2. Someone who leaves or dies takes their grudges with them, silently: no "let go of their grudge" endings for people who are gone.
  3. Mod hooks see each event at the end of the tick it happened in, after all systems have run, so a hook may change the world (it used to crash a system mid-loop).
  4. `RequiredContent` lists the content ids and incident params the code names; `ContentDb` checks them at load, and a test keeps the list in step with the code. An incident with no behaviour is rejected at load.
  5. Food is berries-only until the items milestone: a species that eats another item is rejected at load with a pointer here, instead of starving in play.
  6. Calendar facts come from world.json (late arrivals on the eve of winter; humans sleep from three hours after dusk to dawn). `work_accident` is labelled a minor incident, which is how it has always been paced.
- **Known seams, not yet fixed (from the stranger readers and the architecture review):**
  - Hunger runs on fixed clocks (hungry to starving in exactly six hours; departures exactly a day after starving begins). Per-person appetite and temperament would vary them, but that is a new Truth draw: proposal for the founder.
  - The log's "What happened" shows only notable events, so a refusal "hungry themselves" can appear before any visible hunger line.
  - Blood debts are seldom repaid, so many stories end on a debt nobody returns to.
  - Two near-identical stories (two vampires' first feeding) are told separately, and a story shows one causal path, so a character's earlier deeds (Ilex fighting off thieves before being refused) are left out. Arc detection (fixed decision 8) is the place for this.
  - A spotted thief who reaches the berries before anyone reaches them still steals; it reads oddly when it happens three times in a year.
  - Most stories land in the last ten days (the winter squeeze); the director has no mid-year pressure besides thieves and spoilage.
  - Content fields with no effect yet: items `stackLimit`, incidents `kind` (pacing is in code), traits `sharesFood` (only speeds grudge decay; no food is shared), and work fields for ids whose code does not read them.
  - From the second round of readers (post-fix logs): thefts and spoilage drain the stores but never appear in the winter-hunger stories, because a shortage has no causal memory of what emptied it; when "promised meals come first" leaves someone out, the story cites the policy but not the newcomer's promise that outranked them (Nia's meal and Mira's departure read as unrelated); a refusal can appear without the request before it when only the refusal lies on a story's path.

## ADR-0019 — The art pass: how the dark, grounded look is built (view only)
- **Status:** accepted (2026-11-09), lead's call implementing the founder's ADR-0009 update; for founder review.
- **Context:** The founder asked for the demo's people in option B, "dark and grungy and gorey and more realistic", with Inkscape if possible. The sim and its content formats must not change; the view must stay at 60 fps.
- **Decision:**
  1. **People and animals are procedural** (game/scripts/Art/Figures.cs, ported from the mockup's figureB, wolfB and kingB) so walking, turning and poses animate freely. Pose, facing and held tool come from sim state in Godot-free code (Logic/Poses.cs): walking while moving; a crouch to forage, tend or feed; a swing to fell, mend or fight; sitting to eat, give blood or idle by the hearth; lying when asleep (along the bed, if in one), downed or dead. People turn toward whoever they talk to, from their recent chat, deep-talk, insult or refusal thoughts, and those sitting by the hearth face the fire. Seen from the side a blow raises the tool high in front and strikes down onto what is in front, the blade leading (a test keeps the hand in front of the body). The walk cycle advances with the distance actually drawn, so feet do not slide.
  2. **Textures and decals are SVG sources rendered with Inkscape** (tools/art/src, tools/art/build: ground grunge, grain, dirt, frost, water, planks, masonry, soft blobs, stains, four blood splatters, drops, a pool, scorch, smoke, a soft shadow, the firelight falloff, a vignette). The PNGs in game/art are committed and read from disk at runtime (Image.LoadFromFile on the globalised res:// path), so building or running needs neither Inkscape nor a Godot import step; a missing file is drawn around.
  3. **Appearance the sim does not model** (hair, skin, build, hair style, beard, a cloth override) lives in game/art/looks.json, a view asset keyed by cast key and seeded from the demo's CLOTH and hair tables. Cloth is cast.json's colour unless overridden, darkened and muted at runtime. Vampires are the species that drink blood (collar points, the diamond mark).
  4. **Gore is derived from the sim and never touches it** (Logic/GoreLog.cs). It reads new World.Log events (downings, collapses, work accidents, deaths, bites, sunburn) and, because a fight is logged once while each blow lands silently, it also treats a health drop of 40 or more while fighting as a blow. Only wounds bleed: a collapse or death from hunger or the sun leaves no blood (the sun leaves scorch and a charred, smouldering body). Rotation and choice of splatter come from a hash of the event id (or of the person and tick for a blow), never from the sim's RNG; a test proves a watched world hashes the same as an unwatched one. Marks fade over three in-game days (three times faster in rain); a pool under a body dries but stays. Bodies stay where they died; the badly wounded wear blood-stained clothes and drip as they walk; flies gather by day.
  5. **Every tick the view runs is witnessed by the map** (Logic/ViewStepper.cs, the same loop as Sim.Advance): the warm-up (RETURN_TICKS, tools/play's start day) and the live loop, which runs up to 400 ticks in a long frame. Blows are health drops between looks and marks land where people stand when looked at, so a log that looked once a frame would leave blood that depends on frame timing; a test steps in frames of 1 to 400 ticks and gets the same marks as looking every tick. Stepping through the helper is the only change to SimHost.
  6. **Light:** a CanvasModulate tints the map by time of day (Logic/Daylight.cs, from world.json's dawn and dusk hours, eased; cooler in winter). The night tint (0.34, 0.37, 0.52) is lighter than the mockup's multiply because the grounded palette under it is darker: a night at the camp measures a mean luma of about 25 and a 90th percentile of about 35 (the mockup's night 22.5 and 42). Rain greys the day, but its grey and the wet-ground darkening fade out as night falls, so a rainy dusk is never darker than a dry midnight (a test pins it). At night people catch the moonlight: their cloth, hair and skin are lifted toward a cool light in proportion to the dark (thieves half as much), because a tint that multiplies everything alike cannot make a dark cloak read against the ground. A flickering PointLight2D at the hearth (0.3 + 1.6 × night, 9.5 tiles) matters in proportion to the dark, and the lantern on the watch pole lights while someone keeps watch at night. A vampire burning in daylight glows orange behind the body, with small flames off the shoulders and head. Names, mood marks, rain and a flat vignette are drawn on an unshaded overlay layer above the lit map and below the menu bar.
  7. **Speed:** the Brush triangulates every shape on the CPU and sends one triangle array per batch, with hand-made one-pixel feathered outlines (the Compatibility renderer ignores 2D MSAA); shadows draw in their own pass; the static ground is baked once into a mipmapped texture through an offscreen viewport. Before batching the camp ran at 29 fps; after, about 127 fps with vsync off at night with ten people at ×6 on the founder's M2 (RETURN_FPS=n measures it). After the review fixes the same night scene measures about 106 fps against 112 for the commit before them on the same machine and day (about 0.4 ms more map drawing).
  8. New check hooks for screenshots: RETURN_FOCUS=x,y and RETURN_ZOOM=z (CameraRig), RETURN_FPS=n (FrameProbe).
  9. **Placement, view only:** people sharing a tile stand side by side (0.3 tile apart, in id order, eased so a walker passing through causes a small shuffle); the dead and the downed stay on their blood, a carried patient goes with the bearer. Someone sitting by the hearth sits back from its stones. Someone lying on the ground (not in a bed) lies across the tile's centre, clear of a wall top in front. Everything attached to a person (name, mood mark, hit box, glints, smoke, flies) follows the drawn feet. Names keep off every other figure, stack counts and mood marks: under the feet, else beside them, else one line lower, else wherever overlaps least; the mood mark sits at the upper right of the head and the sleep mark at the upper left.
  10. **Water:** the river is dark teal with a faint, slow tonal drift under crisp ripple marks, drawn in its own layer with mirrored texture repeat (the Inkscape water texture does not tile seamlessly, even with stitched turbulence); the shallows lie in the water and pale toward the bank.
- **Consequences:** Fixed decision 11's "coloured rectangles and text" is superseded for this build by the founder (ADR-0009 update). The wolf and the Barrow King are drawn but unused until the sim has them. Not yet done: a paper-doll cache for figures (each figure is re-triangulated every frame, about 0.3 ms each in a Debug build), the 32 px pawn-sheet check that formally accepts ADR-0009, and fifteen-person frame times (the sim has ten people and the odd thief). Known seams: only people on the same sim tile are spread apart, so two people passing mid-step overlap for a moment, and in a crowded doorway the least-overlap fallback can still put a name on a neighbour's legs; fire, grass and smoke keep moving while the game is paused (the builder's call, so a paused camp still looks alive); tools/art/src/water.svg could be made to tile at source, which would let the river use the texture at full strength.

## ADR-0020 — Fixed decision 12: character growth and arcs (founder addendum)
- **Status:** accepted (2026-11-09), the founder's decision.
- **Context:** The founder supplied a design addendum, "Character Growth and Arcs (fixed decision 12)": companions must visibly change over a lifetime, and static pawns are a failure state.
- **Decision:** Added to CLAUDE.md as fixed decision 12; the full addendum (mechanics, data model, scope, acceptance) is recorded verbatim in BIBLE.md section 5. M0 builds only the data fields and logging (want, arc stage, trait origin), with no director logic; M1 adds trait mutation tables, arc advancement and naming-triggered evolution for two species.
- **Consequences:** Every trait change must reference an incident id and every mutation or evolution roll must use a Dice stream with a purpose tag. The acceptance soak runs 20-year worlds, so aging and multi-year soaks must exist before M1 can be accepted. The story detector will count arc completions as stories once arcs advance (M1).

## ADR-0021 — Growth in M0: trait origins, wants and arcs as data and beats
- **Status:** accepted (2026-11-09), lead's call implementing ADR-0020's M0 scope; the schema additions (wants.json) are approved by the founder's request for this work.
- **Context:** BIBLE.md 5.3: M0 builds the data fields and logging only (want, arc stage, trait origin), no director logic. Many systems read `Character.Traits`, other branches are building alongside, and determinism must not move.
- **Decision:**
  1. **One home for growth state.** `Character.Growth` (`CharacterGrowth`, sim/State/Growth.cs) holds `TraitOrigins`, `Want` and `Arc`, so Character.cs gains one line and later growth fields (evolution tier, memories) need no more. No growth record takes an entity id, so no other id shifts.
  2. **Trait origins run parallel to the trait list.** `Character.Traits` stays a list of ids, so Traits.cs, StateApi, the runner and every system read it unchanged. `TraitOrigins` holds one record per trait, same order and ids: generated (cast.json's signature, the Truth-drawn secondary) or mutated {cause event, tick}. Every trait has a record, not only mutated ones, so a trait changed behind Growth's back breaks a tested invariant (`Growth.InStep`, checked over a year of play) instead of silently reading "born with it". Reads stay lenient (no record reads as generated) so a stale snapshot or a merge never crashes the inspector.
  3. **One door for trait changes:** `Growth.ChangeTrait(w, c, from, to, cause)` gains, loses or replaces a trait (in place, so the signature keeps its slot). It refuses a cause of 0 or an id not in the log, unknown, held or missing traits, and origins out of step; it records the mutated origin and logs a Growth beat citing the cause ("Mira, once Anxious, became Protective."). BIBLE 5.4's "references an incident id" is read as the event that caused the change, whose chain the story log walks back to incidents. Nothing calls it in M0.
  4. **Wants** (content/wants.json, schema content/schemas/wants.schema.json): fourteen camp wants: to be accepted by the camp, to protect {someone}, to keep the camp safe, to earn the camp's respect, a roof of their own (place: the shelter), to be free of every master, to keep their word, never to go hungry again, to provide for others, to find a way home to Earth, to walk in daylight again, to see the stores full before winter (item: berries), to show {someone} what they are worth, someone they can trust. Each has plain words for the inspector (`textKey`, with a `_you` form when it has a pronoun), a beat (`beatKey`, with `_you_a`), a target kind (person, place, item, none) and weights. Places are the camp features every map has (shelter, hearth, stockpile, watch_post, covered_ruins); ContentDb checks every reference and every beat's `_you_a` form at load, and that someone alone can still want something (a want not about a person whose base outweighs all its negative adds).
  5. **Weights add up:** base, plus an add for each trait held, the species, the role and `otherworlder`; a total at or below zero means never. A want for one kind of person takes all its weight from that axis (daylight: vampire 36; home: otherworlder 300); a test proves no cast member can draw them otherwise, whatever their secondary trait. Adding is easy to author and read; the cost is that a gate is a convention that test keeps. Over 1,000 seeds the player wants home in 71% of worlds; each person's signature want leads at 17–36% (Bram provide 36, Oren keep their word 34, Nia never hungry 32, Hollis respect 31, Sera free 31, Mira protect 26 and keep the camp safe 20, Ilex respect 22, Vale daylight 20, Wren trust 17); every type occurs; a camp's ten hold 7.6 distinct wants on average, and in 35% of worlds three or more share one.
  6. **The first want is nature, its target is circumstance.** `Growth.NaturalWant` rolls the weighted pick with `TruthId.InitialWant` (20), keyed by the cast key's FNV-1a hash, over wants.json in file order. `WorldGen.NatureOf` returns it too (`CharacterNature.WantType`), so like the secondary trait it is lazy, order-independent and survives rewinds (tested by reseeding Dice and reversing the arrival order). A want about someone picks, of the others in the camp when it forms, the one they get on with best (a rival: worst) by pair chemistry, a Truth; ties fall to `TruthId.WantTarget` (21). With nobody else in the camp, someone whose natural want is about a person draws again (the same Truth at index 1) among the wants not about a person; everyone else keeps their natural want.
  7. **When a want forms:** when a person enters the world. Camp starters' wants form in `WorldGen.Create`, so state is honest from tick 0, and `Growth.Hourly` logs them on the first tick, because events logged at creation never reach mod hooks and the Chronicle is empty at creation. A newcomer's want forms in `IncidentSystem.Arrive` and its beat cites the arrival. `Growth.Hourly` also gives a want to anyone in the camp who somehow lacks one.
  8. **Arcs:** `Arc {Stage, StalledSinceTick, LastEvent}`; stages want, obstacle, turning point, resolution, failed, abandoned. `Growth.AdvanceArc(w, c, to, cause)` moves exactly one stage on, or to failed or abandoned from any open stage; it refuses no cause, no want, a skip, a step back and any move after an ending. It restarts the stall clock, and an ending sets `Want.Status` (fulfilled, failed, abandoned), the only place that status changes, so want and arc never disagree (the founder's want "state" is `Want.Status`, after `Debt.Status`). The beat cites the cause and the arc's previous beat, so an arc reads as one causal chain from the want forming. Nothing calls it in M0.
  9. **Beats, not stories yet.** Growth beats carry `SystemTag.Growth` and no persistent effect, so none ends a story; the story log lists them under "What happened". M1 adds an effect for arc completion so the analyzer counts arcs (ADR-0020).
  10. **Draw ids:** TruthId 20 and 21 as above. `DiceSystem.Growth = 8` is appended now although M0 draws nothing from it, because `DiceRng` throws on a gap and sibling branches append after it; it costs one unused stream in each snapshot. DiceId 100–119 stay reserved for M1's mutation and evolution rolls.
  11. **Text-state API and view:** `StateApi.Growth(w, id)`, also `CharacterState.Growth` (appended last): each trait with its origin in plain words ("born with it", or "since day 12: Oren was struck down by the thief."), the want in plain words with its target (second person for the player) and status, the arc stage with its label and stall clock. The inspector gives each trait's origin ("Practical — born with it") and a Wants line under Traits ("To find a way home to Earth"), adding the stage once the arc moves ("To protect you (facing an obstacle)").
  12. **Content tests:** the schema test no longer counts content files (it was nine): every schema needs its file and every file its schema, so branches adding files do not collide on a number.
- **Consequences:**
  - Soak, 100 worlds × 1 year, same command before and after: 847 stories both (8.47 per world-year), identical system sets and endings. Every story log is the same apart from 1,000 added want beats (all ten people in every world) and the counts line; in 3 worlds a run of arrivals no longer folds into "… and the same with …", because each arrival's want now sits between "wandered in" and "welcomed". Wants draw Truths only and take no entity id, which is why every Dice sequence and every story is unchanged. Tests: 271 (225 before).
  - `World.SchemaVersion` stays 1: no saved snapshots exist yet. A snapshot from before this change would load with empty growth and eight Dice streams, so `Dice[DiceSystem.Growth]` would throw once M1 draws from it; bump the version when saves arrive.
  - Known seams: arc-stage beats are generic ("Mira found something standing in their way.") and cite the want's beat rather than name the want; M1 can give each want per-stage beat text. Wants are drawn person by person, so a camp can share them (numbers above); the niche rule (BIBLE 5.1.4) is where to spread them. A newcomer who finds the camp empty and is by nature drawn to someone wants something that needs no one instead, so for them alone the formed want differs from `NatureOf`, which assumes company. The player's 71% chance of wanting home is a content number to tune.
- **Review fixes (2026-11-09, a fresh-eyes review of this work):**

  2. The load check that someone alone can still want something counted any want about nothing with a base weight, but adds can cancel a base ("accepted": base 6, Independent −6). Every want's beat also needs its `_you_a` form at load, so a mod's want cannot read "You wants …".
  3. `Growth.ChangeTrait` looked up trait names after changing the list, so a held trait missing from content (a stale snapshot) changed with no beat. Every check and lookup now comes before any change.
  4. `Growth.CanMove` read the arc's order from the enum's values; the forward moves are now spelled out, so adding a stage cannot silently change which moves are allowed.
  5. - **Merge notes:** `DiceSystem.Growth = 8` is also claimed by the build and schedule designs (`Build = 8`, `Schedule = 8`). Nothing draws from it in M0, so it can take any free value at merge as long as the values stay contiguous. `TruthId` 20 and 21 feed the Truths hash: renumbering them is not cosmetic, since it re-draws every person's first want in every world and invalidates the spread measured in point 5, so if a sibling branch's "next free numbers" reach 20–21, renumber the sibling's (none has shipped). `WantDef.Places` is a list in code by choice: the places are map features WorldGen builds, not authored content, and a want about any other place fails at load.

## ADR-0022 — The first four peoples beyond human and vampire: dryads, djinn, fae, undead
- **Status:** accepted for the build (2026-11-09), lead's call on the founder's request ("add mythical and fantasy creatures ... as characters with their own emotions ... just like humans"). Schema changes approved by that request; each is listed here. For founder review.
- **Context:** Fixed decision 5 names four peoples who differ in needs, not stats: dryads need forest, djinn contracts, fae cannot lie, undead need purpose. Each had to be a full character who joins the camp like anyone else, with a need whose mechanics touch at least two systems, thoughts from body and nature, causal events that chain into stories, a seeded scenario test and soak evidence, and the need readable through StateApi. Sim and content only; the next agent gives them their look.
- **Decision:**
  1. **One shared model for needs of a person's nature.** `content/needs.json` (new, with a schema) defines each need: start, decay, low/critical/met marks, low and critical thoughts and events, harm while critical (with its death text), hours critical before they leave, the works or items it can be bound to, and integer params. Shared code (`NatureNeeds`) runs decay, thresholds (events cite whatever last explained the need, like `LastFoodEvent`), thoughts, harm and departure. The need's id names its behaviour, as an incident's does (`Purpose`, `Forest`, `Bargains`, `Courtesy` in `sim/Systems/Natures/`); `RequiredContent.Needs` lists the ids and params the code reads, and an unknown need is rejected at load. A character carries them in one list (`Character.NatureNeeds`); JobPicker asks about critical needs before rest and low ones after. They read as ordinary 0–1000 bars in `CharacterState.Needs`, so the inspector shows them unchanged; `StateApi.NatureNeeds` adds how each stands and what binds it, `StateApi.Contracts` the bargains.
  2. **Schema changes.** species: `foodSource: "none"` (eats nothing from the stores), `tires` (false = never sleeps), `needs`, `unsettlesForDays`, `arrivalKey` (their own arrival line), `refusesWork`, `cannotLie`, `grudgeDecayEveryDays`. cast: `arrivalDays` [first, last]. traits: `refusesUnsafeBargain` (given to Proud, whose description already said it). New files `needs.json` and `loc/en.peoples.json` (all the peoples' text, so en.json is untouched for the parallel branches).
  3. **Undead (the revenants and draugr of European folklore): purpose.** No food, no sleep. Each is bound by a Truth to the work they came back for (keep_watch or forage). Doing it restores purpose, and while it is short of met they lean toward it past a full stockpile or someone else on watch; once met they work like anyone (a first version let a forage-bound revenant forage around the clock and ended winter hunger: 4.8 stories per world). Without it they fade (harm) and lie down at rest. Setting that work to "never" is recorded as the cause, so the player's order heads the chain; winter does the same to one bound to forage. The living take a growing share of a chat's warmth over the undead's first twelve days, and feel uneasy meanwhile.
  4. **Dryads (the tree nymphs of Greek myth): forest.** No food; living trees within reach feed the need (their own tree triple), and when faint they go and stand by their tree (or the thickest grove left). Each is bound by a Truth to one tree near camp, chosen after the nearest three, which the roof takes on Day 1. They never fell a tree whatever their priorities say. Felling any tree grieves them and costs the feller their regard; felling their own makes them cry out, wounds them (Dice), cuts the need and forms a grudge (`EffectKind.BondSevered`). Anyone who thinks well enough of a dryad leaves their tree standing. A felling now cites why the timber was wanted (a promised roof, else a bargain's price) and the feller's grudge against the dryad, if any.
  5. **Djinn (fire spirits of Arabian folklore, as the Nights tell them, with free will and their own ethics; never demons): a bargain.** No food. Unbound, a djinn grows restless and offers a bargain: a service (keep the night watch, or gather berries) for a price each dawn (timber for the fire, or a share of the berries), both Truths, until the season turns. **Who accepts is never the player (fixed decision 2):** the djinn asks whoever it thinks best of (the player only breaks a tie), and they agree by their own nature: their opinion of the djinn against their consent bias, as for blood, and the Proud refuse a bargain the camp could not pay for two dawns. Bargains are first-class (`World.Contracts`; `EffectKind.ContractFormed/Kept/Broken`): paid at dawn before food is shared out (`Bargains.Hourly` runs between needs and rations), kept at the term's end, broken by a dawn with nothing to pay, which turns the djinn against the patron. The camp gathers what open bargains ask for (two days' reserve), so a timber bargain keeps the axes going. Wronged and left unbound, a djinn lets the fire loose once (it burns stockpile timber, and the camp holds it against them); unbound thirty hours, it leaves.
  6. **Fae (the Good Folk of Celtic and wider European folklore): courtesy.** They eat berries. Courtesy moves only by events: any broken promise or bargain in camp stings every fae like cold iron (courtesy, 50 health that never fells them, regard for the breaker), and a broken promise, someone's own word, is held against the oath-breaker as a grudge; kept words, talk and kindness restore it; insults cut it. A day critical and they leave. They cannot sneer: where another would insult, a fae says the hard truth aloud, naming the wrong they hold against the listener so whoever is near hears it (and may turn against them), or says plainly what they think. Their grudges fade a quarter as fast. What a fae owes is sworn and kept before anything else, even their own hunger: welcomed, they swear to repay the host (`DebtKind.Welcome`, carried on the joining event as ADR-0014 does for promises, so a welcome alone is not a story) and bring the host a meal from their own share the moment the host goes hungry. Anyone who gives away their own share now cites the gift as the cause of their hunger.
  7. **Cast** (one each, arriving on a Dice day within their window, drawn after the shuffled wanderers so no other arrival moves; a test proves it): Thessil (dryad, Protective, forager, Days 3–7), Cindral (djinn, Independent, builder: the djinn built for Sulayman, Days 7–12), Varrow (undead, Oathbound, watcher, Days 11–17), Sennet (fae, Proud, carer, Days 14–20). Early enough for each people's story to run through a season and into winter. `game/art/looks.json` had plain placeholder entries for them (replaced by the look; see the update below).
  8. **Food balance.** The fae are one more berry-eater from summer's end, which turned the winter squeeze into a famine (departures 241 → 377 per 100 worlds). A fourth, smaller bush cluster (two bushes, keyed after the first three so none moves) restores ADR-0015's half rations per head; the original ten now leave a little less often than at c818511. Kept in code with ADR-0013's other tuning constants.
  9. **Determinism.** TruthId 30 BoundTree, 31 PurposeWork, 32 BargainService, 33 BargainPrice; DiceId 120 PeopleArrivalDay, 121 PeopleArrivalHour, 122 TreeWound; append-only. New state (`Character.NatureNeeds`, `World.Contracts`, `DebtKind.Welcome`, `JobKind.TendTree/OfferBargain`, the new effect kinds) round-trips in snapshots and counts in the hash (tests: same seed, same world at day 30 with all four in camp; a snapshot at day 21 with bargains open restores and continues to the same hash). Step 1 (eat nothing, never tire) and step 2 (the need model with no needs) left all 100 soak logs byte-identical.

- **Status:** implemented on a branch (2026-11-09), lead's calls; for founder review. - **Decision:**
  1. **Draw ids** are this track's pre-assigned ranges. TruthIds fold the design's thirteen into six, one per entity, with the index naming the fact: `CultureNature = 40` (index 0 name, 1 main faith, 2 + k the k-th custom pick), `FaithNature = 41` (0 name, 1 + k custom pick k), `GodNature = 42` (per god slot: 0 domain, 1 name, 2 epithet, 3 wrath, 4 grace), `PersonBelonging = 43` (0 culture, 1 whether another faith, 2 which, 3 devotion), `SacredTree = 44`, `FestivalDate = 45`; 46 to 49 stay free. A test scans `sim/Systems/Culture/*.cs` and `CultureJobs.cs`: they draw only from the Culture stream, never call `Places.Near`, `JobRunner.RollAccident` or `IncidentSystem.EdgeTile`, and the three culture DiceIds appear nowhere else.
  2. **Schema and state changes:** four content files with schemas (`cultures`, `faiths`, `customs`, `domains`), fourteen thoughts (a separate `RequiredContent.CultureThoughts` list, to keep merges apart), `content/loc/en.culture.json`; `Character` gains `CultureId`, `FaithId`, `Devotion`, `LearnedCustoms`, `Met`; `World` gains `Culture` (a `CultureBook`); `JobKind` gains `Feast` and `RepayDebt`; snapshot `SchemaVersion` 2. `CharacterState` gains `Belonging` as its last field. `cast.json`, `species.json` and `world.json` are untouched.

Everyone but the otherworlder belongs; a people no culture names (the four peoples after the merge) gets a culture through the `"*"` weights, or evenly when every weight is 0 (tested with a mod that adds a dryad).
  4. **Validation** at load names the file and the id: references, ranges, custom kinds and their params, event text for every moment, plurals and stance text for attitude species, one greeting per culture and none in faiths. It is stricter than the design in one place: `customPicks` may not exceed the distinct kinds in the pool that no always-held custom has, so seeding always fills its picks. Festival seasons must exist and last an hour. The custom param accessor is `CustomDef.Arg` (not `Param`, which `RequiredContentCoversIdsNamedInCode` checks against incident params); that test also checks `Arg("...")` names against `RequiredContent.CustomKinds`.
  5. **Kin warmth is +50, not +25.** A chat moves opinion by 1 x warmth / 100 in integers, so +25 changed no opinion at any chemistry; +50 lifts kin at chemistry -1 to 1 and at +1 to 2.
  6. **Guest-bread (H1) is triggered at the top of the job picker and may use picked berries**, not only the host's ration share after their own hunger, as designed. Probes found no keeper holding a share in any of 744 hours a promised guest was left out, and all 19 broken promises in 30 worlds happened at one-berry share-outs (16 of those berries went to the promiser, 3 to people keeping no guest-right), so the designed rule never fired (0 deliveries in 100 worlds). Now a keeper who is neither starving nor injured brings the guest their own share if they hold one ("gave up their own meal") or picks berries ("went out and found ... something to eat"), and a share-out does not break the promise while a host is on the way. The guest's debt to the host cites the kept promise, so one guest-bread is one story, not two. Only a share given away becomes the cause of the host's coming hunger: berries picked for the guest cost the host nothing they would have eaten, and a blood-drinker's hunger is not for berries. A host who finds the bush empty ends without a delivery; the promise then breaks at the next share-out or when due, with no culture event.
  7. **A debt of honour waits two days, not one** (`minDebtAgeHours` 48). At 24 hours repayments ran to 133 per 100 worlds and culture stories to 36.7% of all, over the design's 35% ceiling; at 48 hours, 117 and 34.9%. A debt to a blood-drinker is never settled with berries. A keeper the sun keeps indoors waits for dark, as guest-bread does: the bushes and the stockpile are in the open.

A feast feeds awake berry-eaters, hungriest first, from what nobody has a claim on; with no one to feed it is a lean fire. Festival-goers sit by the fire in the view.
  9. **The sacred tree** may be held by a culture as well as a faith (culture keepers are every member); an unwitnessed felling is remembered as a deed against the felling event.

## ADR-0024 — The main principle: world domination within one lifetime, as a rolled race with one rolled ability
- **Status:** accepted (2026-11-09), the founder's decision.
- **Context:** The founder restated the vision as the game's main principle: you are reincarnated into a world that already has civilizations, cultures, peoples and faiths, as a member of a random race with one unique ability; death returns you to a random point in your past; your goal is to dominate the world by adventuring, gathering followers and friends who grow with you, and building a village and then a civilization; a RimWorld-style storyteller makes every run unpredictable; economy, religion, politics, culture and war cause events through butterfly effects. Asked how it fits the old rules, the founder chose: the unique ability is rolled each run on top of Return by Death, and the win is total domination within one lifetime.
- **Decision:** CLAUDE.md gains a "Main principle" section in the founder's words. The game paragraph is rewritten to match. Fixed decision 3 is amended: old age is still the only death that does not rewind, but it ends the run rather than winning it; the win is total domination of the world within that lifetime. Fixed decision 4 adds the player's race and unique ability, and the civilizations that exist on arrival, to the Truths. The architect-for list gains the world beyond the camp (civilizations, factions, politics, war, economy), the path to domination, and the rolled race and ability.
- **Consequences:**
  - The player's species becomes a Truth draw instead of cast.json's fixed "human", so a run can start as a vampire with a blood need or an undead who needs purpose.
  - A unique-ability system is needed (content-defined, one rolled per run). Each ability must make stories by touching at least two systems, not add a flat stat bonus.
  - The world needs a layer beyond the camp: pre-existing civilizations, factions on the ethics axes, politics, war and an economy (folding in the loot and economy brainstorm).
  - "Domination" needs a measurable definition.
  - Lifespans need to differ by people. Ageless peoples (vampires, undead, perhaps fae) need some other clock, or the lifetime limit means nothing when the player rolls one. This is an open design point.
  - The death-to-fate-knot rewind loop, which today exists only as Truths, Dice and snapshots, must become real gameplay.

## ADR-0025 — Founder sign-off of the Build, Zones, Schedule, Peoples and Culture-and-faith designs
- **Status:** accepted (2026-11-09), the founder's answers. The documents' other recommended answers stand unless the founder objects.
- **Context:** The five designs in docs/superpowers/specs asked 36 questions for the founder between them. The four that only the founder could answer went to him; every other question carries a recommended answer at the end of its document.
- **Decision:**
  1. **Peoples are our own derivatives.** Beyond the archetypes fixed decision 5 names (vampire, dryad, djinn, fae, undead), each people is an original creation inspired by Earth folklore, with its own name, look and twist, and never a reproduction of a tradition's being. There are no paid tradition readers. The content rules stay: no derivative of anything sacred or venerated, or of a belief still used to harm real people, and no caricature. The exclusions in the peoples design apply to derivatives too. An Earth-born player may notice a resemblance ("Earth folklore tells of something like them").
  2. **How people feel about you decides how far your rules carry.** One compliance rule covers zones and schedules. Dislike for whoever set the rule raises the odds of someone going their own way, more so for Independent people. It never applies to the player, and it always leaves a Chronicle line.
  3. **The player starts as a cultural outsider**, with no culture and no faith. They make faux pas until they learn each people's customs, and they keep what they have learned across rewinds.
  4. **Invented faiths may be dark, within limits.** Blood-gifts, taboos, curses, grim rites and feared gods are welcome. For now there are no human sacrifice or torture rites, and nothing mirrors an Earth religion.
  5. **Every other question in the five documents** takes the document's recommended answer.
- **Consequences:**
  - The peoples design is being revised so that batch 2 onward are derivatives with original names.
  - Cast names should come from the world's invented cultures, not real-language name lists (peoples design, question 6), so batch 1's names are reviewed when that branch merges.
  - Build, Zones and Schedule slice 1 start after the peoples and culture branches merge, in the order the designs set.

## ADR-0026 — Founder sign-off of the World, Player and Loot-and-economy designs
- **Status:** accepted (2026-11-09), the founder's answers. The documents' other recommended answers stand unless the founder objects.
- **Context:** The three designs in docs/superpowers/specs asked 11 founder questions between them. The four that shape the game went to the founder; every other question carries a recommended answer at the end of its document.
- **Decision:**
  1. **Domination is held or sworn.** Every standing realm either has its seat held by you or its ruler sworn to you, at one moment while you live. Alliances, a shared faith and trade ties make swearing easier but don't count on their own. Once won, the victory is kept and no return undoes it; you may rule on until old age.
  2. **You die every few years.** About once every three in-game years, through the world's own dangers, which is roughly once per two hours of play at the fastest speed and 12-20 returns in a life. Slice 1 gets there with wounds that bleed until someone tends them, which adds no new draw.
  3. **A life is a soul's span of 40-50 years from arrival**, whatever the body. Ageless peoples keep the same clock. A full life is about 27-30 hours of play at the fastest speed.
  4. **You may direct your own moves**: go to, stay at, seek out. These are intentions for your own character only, and needs can still interrupt them. Everyone else is only ever asked.
  5. **Every other question in the three documents** takes the document's recommended answer. These include the schema and determinism changes of each first slice; war that plunders and burns and kills named people but has no massacres and no slavery; money as goods (silver or salt per world, coin only when a civilization mints one); storied things with a will of their own, kept to blades and a few authored relics; and no food sold to the camp before the food milestone.
- **Consequences:** ADR-0024's two open points are closed: domination is measurable, and the ageless run on the soul's span. The player design's slice 1 becomes the next big piece after Build, Zones and Schedule slice 1, because it makes Return by Death real.
